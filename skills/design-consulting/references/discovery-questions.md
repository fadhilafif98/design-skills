# Discovery Questions

Ask in plain language. **Never** ask about fonts, colors, layouts, or animation. Ask about the business and people, then translate the answers into design yourself.

Max 4 questions per batch. Offer options with a "(Recommended)" guess so the client can answer in one click. If a fact can be inferred from the prompt, codebase, or existing site, confirm it in one line instead of asking.

## Question bank

### The first four (default batch)
1. After visiting, what is the ONE thing they should do? (WhatsApp, book, buy, sign up, call, visit)
2. What's the one thing someone should remember after the first screen? (Every later decision serves this answer.)
3. What makes you different from the place next door? (Offer options typical for the category; the answers seed the inventory.)
4. What do you already have? (logo, brand colors, real photos/video, testimonials, nothing)

### Business
- What do customers say when they recommend you to a friend? (Their words reveal the real positioning.)
- Price position: budget / mid / premium / luxury?

### Audience and scene
- Who is the ideal visitor? (age range, job, situation)
- What are they worried about before buying? (price, trust, quality, time, complexity)
- **Where and when do they look at this?** (on the phone in a queue at noon, at a desk at night, on a big screen in a meeting room). The physical scene decides light vs dark, density, and type size.
- How do they arrive? (Instagram on phone, Google, referral, B2B email)

### Personality
- If your brand were a person, who would it be? (3–4 category-relevant options)
- 3 words you ARE / 3 words you are NOT.

### References (optional)
- 1–3 websites you like (any industry) and the one thing you like about each.
- A website you definitely don't want to look like.

### Constraints
- Deadline, budget, platform (WordPress, Webflow, Next.js, none)?

## Subject inventory (build it, don't ask it all)

Collect 8–12 things that belong to this client and to no competitor. Infer what you can; ask only for what blocks the idea; label the rest CONFIRM.

| Look at | Example |
|---|---|
| Name and what it means | "Catat" = to write down → the shop logs every batch |
| Address, street, neighbourhood, what that place looks like | Jl. Dago 112, colonial tiles, cold morning light |
| Numbers in the business | 1 kg batches, 14 seats, opens 07.00 |
| The constraint it lives with | Roasts only on Tuesdays; closed when it rains (open-air) |
| Materials and colors it touches | Burlap, enamel cups, teak counter |
| Rituals and hours | Regulars ask "what's roasted this week?" |
| Printed things of its world | Roast log, receipt, delivery note, menu board |

When more than three items share one material family, dig until the list spans at least three families. The idea comes from this list, never from the category.

## Answer → design procedure map

Answers set **procedures and constraints**, not looks. A look derived from a category or a personality word ("warm craft → terracotta") is the generated default.

| If the answer is… | Do this |
|---|---|
| Premium / luxury price | Fewer elements per screen, more space, slower narrative motion, the product shown large and real. Type and color come from the subject's own materials, not from "luxury = serif on cream" |
| Budget / value | Prices visible early, high contrast, short copy, fast interface motion |
| Worry = trust | Real faces, places, credentials next to the CTA; no gimmicks; proof only if real |
| Worry = complexity | The process as structure (one section per real step), plain language, a working demo or calculator as the live thing |
| Worry = quality | Macro detail photos, the process proven with real numbers |
| Scene = phone, outdoors, daytime | Light ground, large type, thumb-reach CTA, fast load, vertical media |
| Scene = desk at night / dim room | Dark ground is justified; reduce accent chroma; no glow |
| Arrives via Google | H1 matches the search intent, scannable sections |
| B2B buyers | Credibility near the action, case outcomes, specs/tables; restrained motion |
| Personality words | Become voice rules and motion pace (calm → slower, no bounce; playful → springs allowed). They never pick a palette or a font on their own |
| Has great photos/video | Let media lead: hero media covers ≥ 50% of the first screen |
| Has no photos | Real pictures of the subject's world (its street, materials, archives, with credit), drawings made from the inventory, or committed typography. Never stock people |
| Tight deadline/budget | Fewer sections, one signature moment, Restrained color strategy |

## Turning answers into a brief

```
Purpose:          [audience] should [one action] because [specific value]
Remember:         [the one thing after the first screen]
Scene:            [who, where, device, light] → [light / dark]
Worry to resolve: [worry] → resolved by [section/proof]
Personality:      like [person]; IS [3 words]; IS NOT [3 words]
Inventory:        [8–12 items, CONFIRM where assumed]
Idea:             On this site, [inventory item] becomes [device]
Color strategy:   [Restrained / Committed / Full / Drenched] because [reason]
Must have:        [assets/sections]
Banned:           [tells + client dislikes]
```
